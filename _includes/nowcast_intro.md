## What is this?

GDP measures the total value of what an economy produces. It is the broadest gauge of how the economy is doing, but it arrives late: ISTAT publishes its first estimate for a quarter about 30 days after the quarter ends. Until then, we lack an estimate for how the economy is doing *right now*.

A **nowcast** fills that gap. Every Friday I collect monthly indicators that come out well before GDP release (i.e. industrial production in Italy and Germany, retail sales, exports and imports, unemployment, business and consumer confidence, inflation and interest rates) and use a statistical model to turn them into an estimate of GDP growth in the current quarter. As the quarter goes on and more data arrive, the estimate is updated and usually becomes more precise.

**How to read it.** Growth is measured against the previous quarter, which is how ISTAT and the press report it. A value of 0.25% means GDP is about a quarter of a percent higher than three months earlier; negative values mean the economy is shrinking. Every estimate comes with a range, because it is based on incomplete real-time information.
